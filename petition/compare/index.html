Petition page: the versions side by side

Nothing here is live. Every frame is the real page in demo mode, so looking at it records nothing and none of this lands in the numbers the test is read from. Three things are drawn for independently, so one visitor is in one cell of all three at once and each question is answered on its own.

1. What sits under the Sign button

Four ways, a quarter of visitors each. Everything above the button is identical.

A · growHow fast it is growing A live number for the last seven days, and the feed of who has just signed. Open it on its own
B · quotesWhat other people wrote A signer's own words, changing every seven seconds. No numbers at all. Open it on its own
C · metricHow strongly signers agree The one number we will hand to parliamentarians, live from the answers already given. Open it on its own
D · noneNothing at all The page stops at the button. No proof, no countdown, nothing. The control the other three are measured against. Open it on its own

2. The December line

Three ways, a third of visitors each, drawn separately from everything else.

aboveAbove the button Between the email question and the statement, so it is the last argument before the statement itself. Open it on its own
belowBelow the button Under the button, where it reassures somebody who has already decided rather than delaying somebody who has not. Open it on its own
offNot shown No December line anywhere. The shortest the page gets. Open it on its own

3. How much there is to read above the form

Two ways, half of visitors each, drawn separately from the proof. Top of the page.

fastOne sentence, then the boxes The headline, the sentence that explains the carve-out in plain words, then first name. Open it on its own
bareStraight into the boxes The headline, then first name. Nothing to read above the form at all. Open it on its own
What changed on every version
  • The email question is two buttons and nothing else: yes, email me about this campaign, or no, don't. One has to be chosen.
  • The December line, where it is drawn to go: Parliament votes on this in December, and the data goes to parliamentarians and journalists before the vote.
  • Then the petition statement itself, directly above the button.
  • The proof moved under the button, because what it should say is the thing being tested.
