Our proposed House of Lords amendment to the Public Office (Accountability) Bill.

This is the plain text of the amendment, for anyone who cannot use the briefing page, including readers without JavaScript and anything reading this page automatically.

Schedule 2, page 54, line 24, at end insert—

“Politicians and their functions as public officials

3A (1) “public official” in paragraph 3 also includes a legislator, meaning a member of either House of Parliament, the Scottish Parliament, Senedd Cymru or the Northern Ireland Assembly.

(2) In this paragraph, “politician” means a legislator, or a mayor or government Minister who is a public official under paragraph 3. A politician is a public official only to the extent of their functions as such under paragraph 3(2), with the safeguards and clarification set out below.

(3) When determining whether a politician has committed the offence of misleading the public, their functions as such do not include—

(a) anything said or done as part of proceedings in either House of Parliament, protected from prosecution under this offence by parliamentary privilege under Article 9 of the Bill of Rights;

(b) anything said or done as part of proceedings in the Scottish Parliament, Senedd Cymru or the Northern Ireland Assembly, protected from prosecution under this offence by this paragraph because Article 9 of the Bill of Rights does not apply to those legislatures;

(c) political debate or campaigning, including the expression of opinions or beliefs, however controversial or unpopular;

(d) making an honest mistake, a promise honestly intended to be kept when made but not fulfilled, or an honest prediction that proves incorrect;

(e) solely internal political party administration, without communicating with the public;

(f) conduct concerned solely with private or family life;

(g) private business or professional work unconnected with public functions or the matters in sub-paragraph (6).

(4) The exclusions in sub-paragraph (3)(c) and (d) do not apply where the prosecution proves that the politician intended to mislead the public or was reckless as to whether their act would do so.

(5) For the purposes of the offence of misleading the public, a politician is not a public official in relation to conduct excluded by sub-paragraph (3) and does not commit that offence by engaging in that conduct.

(6) For the purposes of the offence of misleading the public, and subject to sub-paragraph (3), communicating with the public outside proceedings in those legislatures is one of a politician’s functions as a public official under paragraph 3(2).

This includes acts intended to mislead the public, or done recklessly as to whether they will do so—

(a) to start a war, or otherwise influence decisions about military action;

(b) to influence the conduct, campaign or outcome of a referendum held under an enactment;

(c) about the causes of disasters or other events involving death or serious injury;

(d) about risks to public health or safety;

(e) about serious wrongdoing in the exercise of public functions, or the use of public money.

(7) Communications include those made on television, radio, social media or online, at events, or through journalists.

(8) For the purposes of section 12(1), a politician is a public official in relation to conduct within sub-paragraph (6) and acts in that capacity.

(9) In relation to conduct within sub-paragraph (6), psychological harm under section 12(8) includes distress caused to members of the public by that conduct undermining their confidence in the integrity of government or democratic institutions.”
